A one-class anomaly detector for LHC dijet events, built from the Drosophila olfactory circuit. It has no trainable parameters, computes no gradients, and delivers its verdict by walking onto a button.

Circuit

Fifty glomeruli in, one decision out

The fly mushroom body is a novelty detector — not as an analogy, but as a published result. Dasgupta, Stevens & Navlakha (Science, 2017) showed the antennal lobe → Kenyon cell projection is a locality-sensitive hash; Dasgupta, Sheehan, Stevens & Navlakha (PNAS, 2018) showed the mushroom body implements a variant of a Bloom filter. An anomaly search is the same problem: see a great deal of background, then flag what is not like it.

The accident that matters

The antennal lobe applies divisive normalisation, which in the fly makes odour representation invariant to concentration. Transposed to jets, that is exactly pT-scale invariance — so the detector cannot fall into the usual failure mode of learning that “high pT is unusual” and rediscovering the trigger threshold. The fly refuses to do this for reasons that have nothing to do with particle physics.

Results

Measured against real baselines

All three detectors see the same 50 glomeruli and are trained one-class on the same 600,000 background events. Truth labels are used only for evaluation. The fly loses. It is also the only one of the three with nothing to fit.

εS is signal efficiency at the quoted background efficiency. Max SIC is the peak significance improvement, εS/√εB.

The part that matters

Everything here fails where it counts

A discovery does not live at loose cuts. It lives in the tail, after almost all background has been rejected. So the honest question is not the AUC — it is how much signal survives at a given background efficiency, measured against the line random guessing would give you.

All three cross below random at the same place

Every detector beats chance at loose cuts and falls under it once you cut hard, somewhere between εB = 0.10 and 0.05. The most anomalous-looking events under all three methods are not the Z′ — they are unusual QCD. That is why max SIC sits near 1.2–1.4 for the fly, the Gaussian and the autoencoder alike: almost no significance improvement, whatever you fit.

This is not a defect of the fly. It is the known failure mode of unsupervised anomaly detection at a hadron collider, and the fly reproduces it faithfully at a fraction of the parameter count.

Caveats

What is not biology

The steering

A proportional controller on heading error. The real MBON → DAN → descending pathway biases turning, but it is not a P controller, and pretending otherwise would be the one dishonest claim here.

The encoding

A fly does not have a calorimeter. Mapping jet constituents onto 50 glomeruli is our choice; only what happens downstream of it is the animal's.

The verdict

Scores come from the full 1.1M-event run in NumPy. MuJoCo renders a selected handful — you cannot push a million events through a physical fly.

The dataset

LHCO R&D has known labels. The honest next step is the black boxes, where the signal is undisclosed and nobody can tune against it.
